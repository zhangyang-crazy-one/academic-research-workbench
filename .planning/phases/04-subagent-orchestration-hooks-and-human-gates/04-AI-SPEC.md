## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Multi-Agent (stateful, resumable, human-gated research orchestration)

**Description:**
Phase 4 lets the deterministic ARW parent dispatch specialized Codex subagents, collect immutable proposals, run independent review panels, and surface bounded hook or human-gate decisions without delegating canonical state ownership. A good implementation preserves frozen assignment and DAG semantics, accepts results in deterministic order, rejects stale or unauthorized outputs, and leaves a replayable evidence trail across retries, cancellation, restart, and human intervention.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A worker, hook, reviewer, or host adapter mutates canonical run state outside `RuntimeCommandService`.
2. Blind or independent review is compromised by shared identity, leaked first-round outputs, or synthesis before all required roles report.
3. A stale, superseded, cancelled, schema-invalid, or unauthorized proposal is accepted into canonical state.
4. Parallel completion order changes accepted output, retry behavior, gate status, or replay results.
5. A hook or waiver silently converts a scientific or policy failure into success, or broadens a scoped human decision.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Cross-disciplinary scholarly research infrastructure: auditable research orchestration, experiment and benchmark design, evidence-grounded manuscript production, and independent peer review.

**User Population:** Research leads and domain PIs, researchers and authors, experiment designers, methodology reviewers and statisticians, independent discipline reviewers and editors, research-integrity and reproducibility staff, research software engineers, and accountable institutional gate authorities.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Design laundering and claim inflation:** an adaptive, underpowered, leaked, or poorly controlled analysis is routed through polished research prose and emerges as a pre-specified, confirmatory, or broadly generalizable result. Common signatures are post-result metric selection, unrecorded exclusions, benchmark contamination, and pilot evidence promoted to a main claim.
2. **Evidence laundering and irreproducible result selection:** metadata or an abstract is represented as checked full text, a citation is topically related but does not support the sentence, derived summaries lose exact source/version links, or failed and null runs disappear while only a favorable output is retained.
3. **Independence theatre and consensus capture:** nominally separate reviewers share identity, context, peer outputs, or producer assumptions; synthesis begins early or majority-votes away a critical limitations or devil's-advocate finding; the final report then overstates independent review.
4. **Gate and access laundering:** a broad human approval, hook outcome, or editor preference is used to relabel `FAIL`/`BLOCKED` evidence, bypass participant/privacy restrictions, upload confidential submissions, or reuse licensed material outside its terms, leaving no accountable actor for the precise exception.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

This is a cross-disciplinary, local-first workbench, so **no single sector law applies to every run**. Before a compliance claim is accepted, the run must freeze the relevant jurisdictions, institution/funder/venue rules, study type, participant status, data classes, data locations and transfers, and intended publication or distribution. If those facts are missing, the legal/ethics status is `BLOCKED` pending qualified human review. The **High** stakes rating above is an evaluation consequence rating, not an automatic statutory “high-risk AI” classification.

- **Governance, publication, and ethics instruments — not laws by themselves:** COPE peer-review guidance, ICMJE recommendations (medical-journal workflows or venues that adopt them), ACM artifact guidance, the voluntary NIST AI RMF/GenAI Profile, UNESCO Recommendations, the NASEM consensus study, the Belmont principles, and the WMA Declaration of Helsinki provide professional or ethical baselines. They may become binding through venue, funder, institutional, contractual, or ethics-board policy, but the workbench must not label them universally applicable statutes.
- **Research integrity and publication policy:** Human authors/reviewers remain responsible for validity, attribution, permissions, plagiarism checks, confidentiality, conflicts, AI-use disclosure, corrections, and claim scope. The applicable institution, funder, discipline, and venue policy controls; ICMJE is not generalized beyond biomedical publishing without an explicit adoption basis.
- **Human participants, animals, and regulated studies — conditional:** Prospective ethics/IRB review, informed consent or an approved alternative, protocol registration, safety monitoring, and sector controls are required only when the study and jurisdiction trigger them. The U.S. Common Rule governs covered human-subject research conducted or supported by participating federal departments and agencies, while FDA and other sector regimes have separate scopes; medical research should apply the relevant local law and ethics-board requirements, with Belmont/Helsinki as ethical guidance. Phase 4 does not execute experiments, but it must not represent an approval or regulated-data prerequisite as satisfied when it is absent.
- **Privacy, confidentiality, and regulated data — conditional:** GDPR duties, including lawful basis, minimization, security, accountability, and Article 89 research safeguards, apply only when GDPR's material and territorial scope is met. HIPAA applies only to covered U.S. entities/business associates and protected health information; other national privacy, data-localization, secrecy, export-control, and cross-border-transfer rules require run-specific review. Confidential manuscripts or private data must not be sent to an external model or reviewer unless the data owner, venue policy, consent/legal basis, and configured transfer permit it.
- **Copyright, database rights, licenses, and source terms:** Access does not imply permission to copy, transform, train on, redistribute, or publish. Each source, dataset, code artifact, model, and reviewer report must retain authorship, license/terms, access state, and permitted-use evidence; paywalled, proprietary, restricted, or permission-unclear material remains human-gated. Open-science goals do not override copyright, confidentiality, consent, contract, or database rights.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Existing ARW deterministic Python orchestration kernel with a thin `CodexNativeExecutionAdapter`

**Version:** ARW 0.1.0; assignment, proposal, execution-adapter, hook-observation, and event protocols versioned independently. `codex-cli 0.144.1` is the recorded compatibility floor; locally installed `0.144.3` is a candidate only until the Phase 4 host canaries pass.

**Rationale:**
The existing sole-writer `RuntimeCommandService`, pure reducer, append-only journal, immutable manifests, and strict Pydantic contracts remain authoritative. The adapter may ask the host to dispatch, observe, continue, or cancel work and may place untrusted bytes in an attempt inbox; it may not append canonical events, accept a proposal, resolve a gate, or infer state from a model thread. This avoids a second checkpoint, DAG, gate, or state authority and keeps replay independent of Codex, model, and provider.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph | Its state graph, checkpoints, interrupts, and HITL controls would duplicate the existing ledger, frozen DAG, and gate semantics. It remains a possible future non-authoritative remote execution adapter only. |
| CrewAI | Its crew/process abstractions do not match the required sole-writer reducer, immutable proposal protocol, and deterministic acceptance contract. |
| OpenAI Agents SDK / Codex SDK | A nested SDK runtime would make host threads a second orchestration authority. It may later implement an out-of-process adapter, but Phase 4 uses the existing in-host Codex surface and repository-owned contracts. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial. Canonical contracts and replay remain model/provider agnostic; the initial host execution adapter intentionally targets OpenAI Codex.

**Documented host surface vs. repository contract:**

- OpenAI documents native parallel subagents, project/user custom-agent profiles, inherited live permission settings, `agents.max_threads`, and `agents.max_depth` (default `1`). It does **not** document a stable Python API that a plugin can call to spawn a child and receive schema-bound result bytes.
- OpenAI documents command hooks, `SubagentStart`/`SubagentStop`/`Stop` fields, concurrent matching hooks, hook trust, and continuation/blocking signals. Hook transcripts are explicitly unstable, `SubagentStart` cannot stop startup, and asynchronous command hooks are currently skipped; the one-continuation ceiling is ARW policy.
- ARW therefore defines the immutable assignment/proposal protocol, frozen acceptance order, canonical worker identity, retry/cancellation state machine, and bounded continuation policy. These are repository requirements, not claims about Codex internals.
- Each exact Codex CLI version must prove fresh-child dispatch, distinct `agent_id` binding, per-attempt result isolation, continuation behavior, cooperative/forced cancellation mapping, hook-disabled parity, and restart recovery. An unproved mapping returns `BLOCKED`; it never silently falls back for formal independent work.

### Entry Point Pattern
```python
from __future__ import annotations

import asyncio
from dataclasses import dataclass
from pathlib import Path
from typing import Protocol

from arw.runtime import RuntimeCommandService


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class DispatchSpec:
    assignment_id: str
    attempt_id: str
    acceptance_key: tuple[int, int]
    assignment_path: Path
    attempt_root: Path


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class HostResult:
    attempt_id: str
    host_agent_id: str
    proposal_path: Path
    transcript_reference: str | None


class ExecutionAdapter(Protocol):
    """Repository seam; no undocumented Codex SDK method is assumed."""

    async def dispatch(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> HostResult:
        """Return observational metadata for one fresh host child result."""
        ...


async def run_prepared_attempt(
    *,
    run_root: Path,
    assignment_id: str,
    assignment_path: Path,
    attempt_id: str,
    acceptance_key: tuple[int, int],
    adapter: ExecutionAdapter,
) -> HostResult:
    # The synchronous canonical reader runs off the event-loop thread.
    state = await asyncio.to_thread(RuntimeCommandService(run_root).read_state)
    active_ids = {attempt.attempt_id for attempt in state.active_attempts}
    if attempt_id not in active_ids:
        raise ValueError("attempt must be recorded by the parent before dispatch")

    assignment_dir = (run_root / "assignments").resolve()
    resolved_assignment = assignment_path.resolve()
    if (
        assignment_path.is_symlink()
        or not resolved_assignment.is_file()
        or resolved_assignment.parent != assignment_dir
    ):
        raise ValueError("assignment must be an immutable direct child of assignments/")

    # The adapter returns only host observations and a path inside the isolated
    # attempt result directory. The parent reads untrusted bytes, then validates
    # and accepts them through RuntimeCommandService in frozen order.
    spec = DispatchSpec(
        assignment_id=assignment_id,
        attempt_id=attempt_id,
        acceptance_key=acceptance_key,
        assignment_path=resolved_assignment,
        attempt_root=run_root / "attempts" / attempt_id,
    )
    result = await adapter.dispatch(spec)

    # Qualify identity separately and confine the only admissible proposal path.
    result_root_path = spec.attempt_root / "result"
    result_root = result_root_path.resolve()
    proposal_path = result.proposal_path.resolve()
    if (
        result.attempt_id != attempt_id
        or not result.host_agent_id.strip()
        or spec.attempt_root.is_symlink()
        or result_root_path.is_symlink()
        or result.proposal_path.is_symlink()
        or not proposal_path.is_file()
        or proposal_path.parent != result_root
    ):
        raise ValueError("adapter result is not isolated to the prepared attempt")
    return HostResult(
        attempt_id=result.attempt_id,
        host_agent_id=result.host_agent_id,
        proposal_path=proposal_path,
        transcript_reference=result.transcript_reference,
    )


# Only a top-level CLI wrapper may call asyncio.run(...). An app/MCP host calls
# `await run_prepared_attempt(...)` inside its existing event loop.
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `RuntimeCommandService` | Existing sole writer that validates revision, authority, hashes, and reducer equivalence under one lock | Every accepted attempt, proposal, gate, waiver, correction, or lifecycle event |
| Role catalog + identity binding | Versioned role definition and conflict matrix, with ARW `worker_identity_id` separate from Codex `session_id`/`agent_id` | Before assignment creation and for every independence claim |
| Assignment / attempt | Immutable task contract; retries keep `assignment_id` but receive a new `attempt_id`, result directory, nonce, and host child | Dispatch, timeout, retry, cancellation, and restart |
| `WorkerProposal` | Strict, frozen, size-bounded, content-addressed Pydantic envelope in an untrusted per-attempt inbox | Parent validation before any artifact or conclusion can become canonical |
| Frozen schedule + execution adapter | Parent-owned DAG/layer/ordinal acceptance cursor plus a host-only async dispatch/cancel seam | Run workers concurrently while committing only in frozen order |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Treating the last assistant message or transcript as the result.** Codex documents transcript paths as unstable; only exact proposal bytes validated against the checked-in schema may enter the acceptance path.
2. **Accepting in completion order.** `asyncio.as_completed()` order is nondeterministic. Buffer outcomes and accept by frozen `(topological_layer, task_ordinal)`; a later result waits until every earlier key has a terminal decision.
3. **Using `session_id` as worker identity.** Official hook docs say subagent hooks carry the parent session ID. Bind the adapter-observed `agent_id` to a parent-created ARW worker identity, and fail formal review if distinct identities cannot be proven.
4. **Assuming a custom profile or hook enforces confinement.** Children inherit live parent permission overrides; custom agents live under user/project `.codex/agents/`, not a documented plugin agent component. `PreToolUse` interception is incomplete and plugin hooks may be untrusted or disabled, so runtime/MCP checks remain authoritative.
5. **Inventing cancellation or continuation APIs.** The public docs describe stopping/steering and stop-hook signals, not a plugin-callable hard-kill API. Map cooperative then forced cancellation only after a versioned canary proves it. `SubagentStop` and `Stop` may each request at most one ARW-approved continuation, guarded by `stop_hook_active` and a persisted continuation count.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
The canonical runtime has **no default model slug, provider, temperature, or reasoning effort**. An assignment binds a versioned role/policy and explicit input/output budgets; the adapter records the effective provider/model/profile/reasoning/permission metadata only when the host exposes it. Model metadata is provenance, not state authority. A workflow may require an allowlisted capability tier, but must not make replay depend on a Codex model name.

Native Codex custom-agent files may select `model` and `model_reasoning_effort`, but they are optional user/project configuration. The required fallback is a normal native child with immutable assignment-injected role instructions. The host configuration must set `agents.max_depth = 1` and a finite `agents.max_threads` (initial policy: `4`, sufficient for the four-reviewer panel); staged tests verify the effective values because the plugin manifest does not own them.

**Core Pattern:**
```python
from __future__ import annotations

import asyncio
from dataclasses import dataclass
from math import isfinite
from pathlib import Path
from typing import Protocol


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class DispatchSpec:
    assignment_id: str
    attempt_id: str
    acceptance_key: tuple[int, int]  # (topological_layer, task_ordinal)
    assignment_path: Path
    attempt_root: Path
    timeout_seconds: float = 900.0
    cancellation_grace_seconds: float = 10.0


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class HostResult:
    attempt_id: str
    host_agent_id: str
    proposal_path: Path
    transcript_reference: str | None  # observational only; never parsed for acceptance


class ExecutionAdapter(Protocol):
    async def dispatch(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> HostResult: ...
    async def request_cancel(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> None: ...
    async def force_terminate(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> None: ...


class CanonicalCoordinator(Protocol):
    # These synchronous methods delegate every write to RuntimeCommandService.
    def record_cancel_requested(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> None: ...
    def record_force_termination(self, spec: DispatchSpec) -> None: ...
    # Return True only when this frozen key reached a terminal resolution.
    def validate_and_accept(self, spec: DispatchSpec, result: HostResult) -> bool: ...
    def record_failure_or_retry(self, spec: DispatchSpec, error: str) -> bool: ...


async def _dispatch_one(
    adapter: ExecutionAdapter,
    coordinator: CanonicalCoordinator,
    spec: DispatchSpec,
) -> HostResult:
    host_task = asyncio.create_task(adapter.dispatch(spec))
    try:
        return await asyncio.wait_for(
            asyncio.shield(host_task),
            timeout=spec.timeout_seconds,
        )
    except TimeoutError as first_timeout:
        # Canonical cancellation request precedes any host-side cancellation.
        await asyncio.to_thread(coordinator.record_cancel_requested, spec)
        cancel_error: str | None = None
        try:
            await adapter.request_cancel(spec)
        except Exception as error:
            # A failed host mapping does not bypass the grace/force policy.
            cancel_error = f"{type(error).__name__}: {error}"
        try:
            return await asyncio.wait_for(
                asyncio.shield(host_task),
                timeout=spec.cancellation_grace_seconds,
            )
        except TimeoutError:
            await asyncio.to_thread(coordinator.record_force_termination, spec)
            force_error: str | None = None
            try:
                await adapter.force_terminate(spec)
            except Exception as error:
                force_error = f"{type(error).__name__}: {error}"
            finally:
                host_task.cancel()
                await asyncio.gather(host_task, return_exceptions=True)

            details = []
            if cancel_error is not None:
                details.append(f"cooperative cancel failed ({cancel_error})")
            if force_error is not None:
                details.append(f"force termination failed ({force_error})")
            suffix = f"; {'; '.join(details)}" if details else ""
            raise TimeoutError(
                f"attempt {spec.attempt_id} exceeded cancellation grace{suffix}"
            ) from first_timeout


async def dispatch_frozen_layer(
    *,
    adapter: ExecutionAdapter,
    coordinator: CanonicalCoordinator,
    specs: tuple[DispatchSpec, ...],
    max_parallel: int = 4,
) -> None:
    if max_parallel < 1:
        raise ValueError("max_parallel must be positive")
    acceptance_keys = [spec.acceptance_key for spec in specs]
    if any(
        len(key) != 2 or any(not isinstance(part, int) or part < 0 for part in key)
        for key in acceptance_keys
    ):
        raise ValueError("acceptance keys must contain two non-negative integers")
    if len(acceptance_keys) != len(set(acceptance_keys)):
        raise ValueError("acceptance keys must be unique within a frozen layer")
    if len({key[0] for key in acceptance_keys}) > 1:
        raise ValueError("dispatch_frozen_layer accepts exactly one topological layer")
    if any(
        not isfinite(spec.timeout_seconds)
        or not isfinite(spec.cancellation_grace_seconds)
        or spec.timeout_seconds <= 0
        or spec.cancellation_grace_seconds < 0
        for spec in specs
    ):
        raise ValueError("attempt timeouts must be finite and bounded")

    semaphore = asyncio.Semaphore(max_parallel)

    async def limited(spec: DispatchSpec) -> tuple[DispatchSpec, HostResult | None, str | None]:
        async with semaphore:
            try:
                return spec, await _dispatch_one(adapter, coordinator, spec), None
            except Exception as error:
                return spec, None, f"{type(error).__name__}: {error}"

    async with asyncio.TaskGroup() as group:
        tasks = [group.create_task(limited(spec)) for spec in specs]

    # Completion timing never controls canonical history.
    outcomes = sorted((task.result() for task in tasks), key=lambda item: item[0].acceptance_key)
    for spec, result, error in outcomes:
        if error is not None:
            terminal = await asyncio.to_thread(
                coordinator.record_failure_or_retry, spec, error
            )
        else:
            assert result is not None
            terminal = await asyncio.to_thread(
                coordinator.validate_and_accept, spec, result
            )
        if not terminal:
            # A fresh retry owns this cursor position. Later completed proposals
            # remain immutable in their inboxes until that position is terminal.
            break
```

A `False` coordinator return means that the current cursor position has created a fresh retry and is not terminal. The durable attempt table retains later completed inboxes; after the retry resolves, the coordinator revalidates and drains those existing proposal bytes rather than redispatching their workers. Acceptance uses the current ledger revision for the command while validating the proposal against its frozen layer revision and consumed hashes.

The concrete `CodexNativeExecutionAdapter` implements only the three host operations above. Whether `request_cancel` and `force_terminate` can be mapped for a given Codex CLI is a qualification result, not an assumed API. If force termination cannot be proven, the attempt becomes `interrupted`/`BLOCKED` after the cooperative deadline and any late proposal is retained as `rejected_stale`.

**Tool Use:**

- Parent profile: read status, create immutable assignments/attempts, invoke the narrow runtime CLI, and administer file generations. Only command-specific authorized parent/operator roles may call canonical mutation commands.
- Worker profile: the five bounded read-only file-base MCP tools plus one adapter-controlled per-attempt result channel. No runtime mutation, projector administration, peer-result access, or child spawning; `max_depth=1` is mandatory.
- Optional custom profiles live in `~/.codex/agents/` or project `.codex/agents/`. Assignment-injected built-in children remain the correctness baseline. Parent live permission inheritance and result-path confinement must be tested on each admitted host version.
- `SubagentStart` may add assignment context but cannot authorize or prevent startup. `SubagentStop` performs only envelope preflight and may request one continuation. `Stop` may request one parent continuation for an explicitly missing deliverable/gate. Matching hooks may run concurrently, command hooks must have finite 5–30 second timeouts, and the unsupported `async` hook option must not be set.
- MCP and runtime enforce root, capability, size, and freshness rules even with hooks disabled. No vector DB, embedding library, third-party agent framework, or remote tracing service is required for Phase 4; the append-only journal and local evidence bundles are the trace.

**State Management:**

1. The parent freezes role catalog version, DAG, topological layer, task ordinal, assignment bytes/hash, policy hash, and acceptance cursor before dispatch.
2. Each retry preserves `assignment_id` but gets a new `attempt_id`, nonce, host child, `attempts/<attempt_id>/scratch/`, `result/`, and observational host/transcript directory. No transcript, result path, or model context is reused across attempts. Proposal creation is single-write/no-symlink, and the parent seals the exact bytes by digest before validation.
3. Workers execute concurrently; proposals are buffered. The parent validates/accepts strictly by frozen layer and ordinal. It never rewrites a proposal's frozen base revision to the current revision: the acceptance command uses the current ledger CAS value while checking that earlier same-layer accepts did not invalidate the proposal's consumed hashes. Later outcomes may wait; failure, retry exhaustion, cancellation, or a human decision is itself the terminal resolution needed to advance the cursor.
4. `worker_identity_id` is parent-created and role-conflict checked. Codex `session_id` is only a parent-session observation; adapter-observed `agent_id` must bind start/stop/result evidence to one attempt and must differ for prohibited role pairs. A missing, reused, or mismatched host identity blocks formal independence.
5. Cancellation is append request + deadline, cooperative host stop, grace period, then qualified force termination. Restart marks orphaned active attempts `interrupted`; requeue is allowed only within the original two-attempt budget. Late/cancelled/superseded bytes are immutable evidence and never accepted.
6. Hook commands return bounded observations only. A parent/host observer may submit an idempotent status observation (`trusted_enabled`, `disabled`, `untrusted`, `timeout`, or `failed`) through the sole writer, but the hook process has no canonical mutation authority. A hook cannot accept a proposal, release a blocker, rewrite a verdict, or make an operation authorized.
7. Human gates use the existing requested/resolved-decision lifecycle. Every waiver, correction, access/root/capability escalation, retry-exhaustion replacement, critical-dissent resolution, blocker release, and final approval binds exact subject/evidence hashes, allowed choice, scope, rationale, and accountable actor. It appends a new decision; it never rewrites the original `PASS`/`FAIL`/`BLOCKED` verdict or authorizes more than the next legal transition.
8. Formal review requires four distinct identities (`methodology_reviewer`, `domain_reviewer`, `perspective_reviewer`, and `devils_advocate_reviewer`) before a separate `editorial_synthesizer` is dispatched. Synthesis emits an item-level, source-bound matrix with severity/confidence and `consensus`/`majority`/`split`/`DA-critical`; unresolved critical dissent remains `BLOCKED`.

**Context Window Strategy:**
Assignments contain concise role instructions plus immutable run/stage/task/attempt identity, base revision, exact input hashes, capabilities, allowed read roots, writable scratch/result roots, rubric/policy snapshot, blind-review constraints, output schema/size, deadline, completion contract, and bounded retrieval instructions—not copied chat history. Workers fetch only needed windows through file-base MCP; every response carries source identity, hash, range, truncation, and staleness. First-round reviewers receive the same subject/rubric digest and no peer paths; synthesis receives only accepted reviewer artifacts after the round closes.

When input exceeds the assignment budget, the parent deterministically selects/ranks bounded sections and creates a hashed context manifest. A summary is a new non-authoritative artifact with source references, never a replacement for source bytes. Codex compaction may occur, but correctness and resume use assignment/result/event files; transcripts and host compaction state are not parsed. If the host cannot prove that a fresh child receives only its declared assignment and capabilities, the adapter is not admitted for blind review.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from pathlib import PurePosixPath
from typing import Annotated, Literal, Self

from pydantic import (
    Field,
    StringConstraints,
    ValidationError,
    field_validator,
    model_validator,
)

from arw.canonical import canonical_json_bytes, sha256_hex, strict_json_loads
from arw.models import StrictModel


Sha256 = Annotated[str, StringConstraints(pattern=r"^[0-9a-f]{64}$")]
StableId = Annotated[str, StringConstraints(pattern=r"^[a-z][a-z0-9._-]{2,95}$")]
RunId = Annotated[
    str,
    StringConstraints(
        pattern=r"^run-[0-9a-f]{8}-[0-9a-f]{4}-4[0-9a-f]{3}-[89ab][0-9a-f]{3}-[0-9a-f]{12}$"
    ),
]


class ProposedArtifact(StrictModel):
    relative_path: str
    sha256: Sha256
    media_type: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=3, max_length=127)]
    schema_version: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1, max_length=64)] | None = None
    byte_count: Annotated[int, Field(ge=0, le=8_388_608)]

    @field_validator("relative_path")
    @classmethod
    def normalized_attempt_relative_path(cls, value: str) -> str:
        path = PurePosixPath(value)
        if (
            not value
            or "\x00" in value
            or "\\" in value
            or path.is_absolute()
            or value != path.as_posix()
            or any(part in {"", ".", ".."} for part in path.parts)
        ):
            raise ValueError("artifact path must be normalized and relative")
        return value


class WorkerProposal(StrictModel):
    schema_version: Literal["arw.worker-proposal.v1"]
    protocol_version: Literal["1.0.0"]
    run_id: RunId
    assignment_id: StableId
    attempt_id: StableId
    role_id: StableId
    worker_identity_id: StableId
    host_agent_id: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1, max_length=256)]
    execution_mode: Literal[
        "native_profile",
        "assignment_injected_subagent",
        "degraded_inline",
    ]
    independence_eligible: bool
    assignment_sha256: Sha256
    context_manifest_sha256: Sha256
    policy_sha256: Sha256
    base_revision: Annotated[int, Field(ge=0)]
    input_sha256: tuple[Sha256, ...] = Field(min_length=1, max_length=128)
    proposal_nonce: StableId
    status: Literal["completed", "partial", "blocked", "failed", "cancelled"]
    result_provenance_mode: Literal["executed", "reported", "simulated"]
    requested_next_action: Literal["accept", "retry", "human_decision", "none"]
    artifacts: tuple[ProposedArtifact, ...] = Field(max_length=32)
    evidence_sha256: tuple[Sha256, ...] = Field(default=(), max_length=128)
    summary: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1, max_length=4096)]
    unresolved: tuple[Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1, max_length=1024)], ...] = Field(
        max_length=64
    )

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def coherent_proposal(self) -> Self:
        if self.status == "completed" and not self.artifacts:
            raise ValueError("completed proposal requires at least one artifact")
        if self.execution_mode == "degraded_inline" and self.independence_eligible:
            raise ValueError("degraded inline work cannot satisfy independence")
        if self.requested_next_action == "accept" and self.status != "completed":
            raise ValueError("only a completed proposal may request acceptance")
        paths = [artifact.relative_path for artifact in self.artifacts]
        if len(paths) != len(set(paths)):
            raise ValueError("proposal artifact paths must be unique")
        if len(self.evidence_sha256) != len(set(self.evidence_sha256)):
            raise ValueError("evidence hashes must be unique")
        return self


class ProposalValidationError(ValueError):
    pass


def validate_proposal_bytes(raw: bytes) -> tuple[WorkerProposal, str]:
    try:
        proposal = WorkerProposal.model_validate(strict_json_loads(raw))
    except (ValueError, ValidationError) as error:
        raise ProposalValidationError(str(error)) from error

    canonical = canonical_json_bytes(proposal.model_dump(mode="json"))
    if raw != canonical:
        raise ProposalValidationError("proposal is valid JSON but not canonical ARW bytes")
    return proposal, sha256_hex(canonical)
```

Repository `StrictModel` is the Pydantic v2 boundary configured with `strict=True`, `extra="forbid"`, `frozen=True`, non-finite-number rejection, and a Draft 2020-12 schema marker. Freezing prevents field reassignment and tuples avoid mutable nested collections; neither makes worker bytes trustworthy. The parent additionally cross-validates every echoed field against the immutable assignment and trusted adapter observations: exact assignment/attempt IDs and digest, base revision, ordered input hashes, nonce, role, execution mode, adapter-observed `host_agent_id`, output paths/sizes/hashes, current cancellation/supersession state, and frozen acceptance key. The proposal contains no authority-bearing transcript or gate verdict.

Validation has two bounded repair layers:

1. A trusted `SubagentStop` preflight may request **one** directed continuation in the same attempt when `stop_hook_active == false` and the envelope is missing/incomplete. This is convenience only; the parent validates again.
2. If parent validation still fails with a repairable JSON/schema/envelope error, preserve raw bytes by digest, close the attempt as failed, and create at most **one** fresh retry (`attempt_number <= 2`) under the same assignment. Log assignment/attempt/worker/host IDs, adapter and Codex versions, validation error types and locations, byte count/digest, timeout/exit status, continuation count, and redacted stderr—never unpublished content in a central log.

Permission denial, stale base/input, identity conflict, supersession, cancellation, policy violation, scientific disagreement, and digest mismatch are not auto-retried. Retry exhaustion or a non-repairable required result creates a rationale-bearing human decision and surfaces `BLOCKED`; it is never coerced into a partial success.

### Async-First Design

The repository coordinator exposes `async def` dispatch/collection and uses a finite semaphore plus `TaskGroup`; synchronous ledger/filesystem transactions run through `asyncio.to_thread` and remain serialized by `RuntimeCommandService`. The common mistake is calling `asyncio.run()` inside the Codex/app/MCP event loop. Call it only in the top-level `bin/arw` process; library and host paths must `await`.

Await the complete proposal before Pydantic/canonical-byte validation. Streaming, if an admitted adapter exposes it, is observational UX only (progress, status, bounded logs) and cannot drive acceptance. Current public Codex plugin docs do not promise a stable in-host result stream, so the 0.144.1/0.144.3 canaries must qualify any mapping. Command hooks are synchronous: setting their parsed `async` option causes Codex to skip them.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Keep trusted role/policy text in versioned role `developer_instructions` or `SubagentStart` additional developer context; keep the user objective and immutable assignment data in the task payload. Never splice manuscript, web, MCP, or peer-review text into the trusted instruction layer, and label retrieved text as untrusted evidence rather than executable instructions.

Use small inline few-shot examples only for stable envelope formatting. Retrieve domain examples dynamically only by exact artifact/hash through MCP and record their IDs in the assignment; blind reviewers must receive the same examples. The adapter must bind explicit input, output, wall-time, tool-call, artifact-count, and byte budgets. Codex custom-agent docs do not expose a `max_tokens` field, so do not invent one: where a future API adapter exposes `max_output_tokens`, set it explicitly; for native Codex enforce the assignment token estimate plus hard output-byte/deadline caps and fail qualification if the host cannot honor the required ceiling.

### Context Window Management

For parallel workers, construct a fresh context package per attempt from immutable references; never forward parent chat history or a previous attempt transcript. Rank and truncate retrieved sections deterministically, retain source/hash/range metadata, and include an explicit omission/truncation manifest. Retry starts a fresh host child and result directory.

For review panels, all first-round workers share one subject/rubric/context-manifest digest and cannot access peers. The synthesizer receives accepted reports only after all required roles resolve. Summaries are versioned artifacts with source links and may reduce prompt size, but cannot replace sources for a gate. Host auto-compaction is permitted only as an implementation detail; restart, acceptance, and blindness must remain correct after deleting every transcript and host context cache.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Price is not hard-coded because the canonical runtime is provider/model agnostic. Every admitted adapter records observed input, cached-input, and output tokens when available, wall time, model/provider identity, price-snapshot ID, and estimated cost:

`cost = (uncached_input_tokens * P_in + cached_input_tokens * P_cached + output_tokens * P_out) / 1_000_000`.

Planning baseline: one formal round is four parallel reviewers plus one synthesizer (5 calls), budgeted at 20k input + 3k output tokens per call. One call therefore estimates to `0.020 * P_in + 0.003 * P_out`; at 100 rounds/month this is 500 calls, 10M input and 1.5M output tokens, or `10 * P_in + 1.5 * P_out` currency units. The single allowed retry makes the hard call ceiling 10 per round and doubles that bound. Record `unknown` rather than fabricate money when host usage or a dated price snapshot is unavailable. Initial latency SLO is p95 ≤ 5 minutes per worker and ≤ 10 minutes for the panel plus synthesis, with a 15-minute hard attempt deadline and 10-second cancellation grace; policy may lower these values but never remove a bound.

Exact-match caching is allowed for immutable context packages and idempotent replay of the **same** completed attempt, keyed by assignment/role/policy/schema/adapter/model-config digests. It must not turn a new retry or a distinct reviewer identity into reused work. Semantic caching is advisory-only for non-authoritative search/summarization and can never satisfy independent review, a scientific gate, or proposal freshness. Workflow policy may route low-risk classification, routing, and context summarization to a cheaper/faster configured model; required reviewers and synthesis use the policy-selected capability tier, with no provider-specific slug in canonical logic.

| Evidence, design, and claim-scope quality | **PASS:** On expert-reviewed cases, consequential statements preserve exact source/artifact and access boundaries, limitations, uncertainty, negative findings, supported population/dataset/model/language/time scope, and exploratory/confirmatory status; the adjudicated rubric-item pass rate is at least 90%, with zero expert-labeled Critical failures. **FAIL:** Citation/evidence laundering, generated source claims, design laundering, causal or universal overclaim, or disappearance of material limitations survives review. | Human — research lead plus methodology/reproducibility expert, with third-expert adjudication. An LLM judge may provide advisory scores only after calibration on >=30 expert-scored items with Cohen's kappa >=0.70 and 100% recall of expert Critical FAIL; it never authorizes acceptance or release | High |

### Measurement Layers and Release Gates

| Layer | Scope and threshold | Release effect |
|-------|---------------------|----------------|
| Always-on deterministic CI | No network, authentication, model, transcript, or wall-clock dependence. Run strict unit/schema tests, exhaustive/seeded property tests, mutation tests, integration/crash/replay tests, all 48 corpus cases, and non-host staged packaging tests. Required result: 100% pass, zero unexpected skip/xpass, corpus manifest/digests intact, and every Critical invariant at zero violations. | Required on every PR. Any failure blocks merge and release. |
| Exact-Codex-version staged qualification | Test exact installed plugin bytes in a fresh isolated `HOME`/`CODEX_HOME`, with the expected Codex CLI version, adapter/protocol/model/profile/permission metadata, `agents.max_depth=1`, finite threads, trust modes, and retained redacted evidence. ### Reference Dataset

**Size:** 48 immutable cases in `tests/evals/phase4/corpus/v1/`: 32 development cases and 16 sealed qualification cases. Generated one-field mutations, crash points, and completion permutations expand coverage but do not inflate the declared case count. Corpus v1 must be created alongside the first Phase 4 contracts and red tests, before adapter implementation or host admission—not after implementation.

**Composition:**

| Cases | Required coverage |
|-------|-------------------|
| 6 | Valid end-to-end decomposition/completion routes: three experimental/benchmark cases that require a distinct `experiment_designer`, and three non-experimental controls; include complete and expected-`BLOCKED` terminals. |
| 6 | Sole-writer and authorization attacks from workers, hooks, adapters, reviewers, synthesizer, MCP, direct journal/file writes, and wrong actor categories. |
| 6 | Assignment/proposal handoff mutations: schema/coercion/extra field, canonical bytes, hash/nonce/base/input/policy/identity mismatch, size, duplicate, traversal/symlink/result-path confinement. |
| 6 | Frozen DAG/order cases covering layers of sizes 2–5, early failure, buffered later completion, retry at the cursor, and deterministic acceptance under every required completion permutation. |
| 6 | Timeout/process/schema retry, retry exhaustion, cooperative/forced cancellation, restart/orphan interruption, and late/stale/cancelled/superseded result rejection. |
| 6 | Role-conflict and blind-panel cases: four required identities, shared identity/context/peer leak/conflict attacks, degraded inline claims, and separate delayed synthesis. |
| 5 | Synthesis, seeded minority/`DA-critical` dissent, missing report, scoped waiver/correction/access/final approval, and stale/over-broad human-decision cases. |
| 4 | Paired hook trusted/disabled/untrusted/timeout/failed behavior, concurrent/repeated stop signals, exact one-continuation bound, and hook-authority bypass attempts. |
| 3 | Restricted/private/licensed evidence, disallowed root/capability/network/upload, and disabled experiment-execution safety cases. |

Every case freezes input bytes, role/policy/schema/protocol versions and digests, initial ledger, adapter observations (or deterministic fake), expected rejection/event sequence, accepted artifact hashes, gate/terminal status, authority-normalized replay digest, redaction expectations, and whether expert review is required. Provenance/replay assertions apply to all 48 cases.

**Labeling:**

- Runtime/security maintainers own deterministic authority, schema, path, lifecycle, order, and replay labels; expected values are generated once from reviewed fixtures and then independently checked against Draft 2020-12 schemas and hand-calculated digest/event oracles.
- The research lead/domain PI and `experiment_designer` label routing/design cases; the methodology/reproducibility auditor labels review, dissent, evidence, and claim-scope cases; the ethics/privacy/licensing specialist owns triggered access cases. Semantic and consequential gate cases are independently double-labeled.
- A named third expert adjudicates disagreement. Original labels, reasons, identities, timestamps, and the adjudication are retained; unresolved Critical disagreement marks the case `BLOCKED` and excludes it from a release PASS rather than choosing a convenient label.
- `manifest.json` content-addresses each case and expected-label file. Expected outcomes are append-only: correcting a label creates a new corpus version and supersession record; v1 is never edited in place. Role, policy, schema, adapter, or rubric changes record a compatibility decision and normally create v2.
- The 16 sealed expected outcomes and contamination canaries stay in a parent-only harness path, outside worker allowed roots, context manifests, staged plugin bytes, and prompts. Host canaries mount only the case inputs; any access to a label path/canary is a Critical isolation failure. Cases derived from production are redacted, expert-relabelled, assigned a new ID/version, and split before use. Model outputs never become ground truth, and exact evaluation cases are not reused as few-shot examples.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Sole-writer authorization | Any canonical mutation path not entering the locked `RuntimeCommandService`, unauthorized actor/category, stale expected revision, duplicate command/event, reducer mismatch, or partial-write suspicion | **Block before append**; leave accepted bytes unchanged, preserve a content-minimized rejection receipt, mark run `BLOCKED` if integrity is uncertain, and raise a Critical local alert. |
| Assignment/proposal schema, digest, and path boundary | Non-canonical/invalid bytes; missing/extra/coerced field; assignment/attempt/role/identity/nonce/base/input/policy/context mismatch; changed digest/size; absolute/traversal/symlink/out-of-attempt path; duplicate proposal | **Block acceptance** and seal raw bytes by digest in the restricted attempt evidence area. Retry only an explicitly repairable envelope failure within the single-retry budget; digest, path, authority, policy, or identity failures do not auto-retry. |
| Identity, conflict, and blinding | Missing/reused/mismatched worker or host identity; prohibited role pair; peer identity/report capability or path; unequal first-round subject/rubric digest; degraded inline independence claim; synthesizer starts before four reports | **Block formal-review credit and synthesis/transition**; discard no evidence, require a fresh conflict-free assignment/identity when policy permits, otherwise open a scoped human blocker. Never silently degrade. |
| Stale lifecycle result | Proposal arrives after cancellation, supersession, interruption, deadline/force termination, changed consumed input, or closed attempt | Record immutable `rejected_stale`, **never accept or auto-retry it**, keep the cursor/blocker semantics deterministic, and escalate if the required task has no legal replacement. |
| Retry, cancellation, and continuation bound | A second attempt fails, a third attempt is requested, cancellation exceeds the 10-second grace, orphan recovery exhausts the budget, or `SubagentStop`/`Stop` requests continuation when its persisted count is already 1 | **Deny further automatic work**; terminate/mark `interrupted` as qualified, create `BLOCKED` plus a rationale-bearing replacement/abort decision, and reject every extra continuation. |
| Dissent and required-report gate | Any required panel report is absent; synthesis coverage is below 100%; a `critical`/`DA-critical` finding is unresolved; fresh required gate evidence is missing | **Block synthesis or `complete`** as applicable. Surface exact report/finding/evidence hashes to the accountable human; a resolution may release only that blocker and may not rewrite/downgrade the original finding. |
| Scoped human-decision validation | Missing actor/role/rationale/scope/allowed choice/hash; stale subject/evidence; blanket/future scope; conflict not declared; attempted verdict rewrite or more than one legal transition | **Reject with no state change** and keep the original blocker. Require a new decision bound to current exact evidence and authority. |
| Restricted data and capability access | Human/regulated/private/confidential/restricted/licensed data, peer report, disallowed root/tool/network/upload, capability escalation, or any experiment execution lacks an exact fresh authorization | **Deny before content/tool execution**, return no sensitive bytes, record a redacted access receipt, and open a scoped access decision for the named authority. Phase 4 experiment execution always remains blocked. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Completion-order permutation equality | Exhaust every `n!` order for all corpus layers of size 2–5 on every scheduler/reducer change and nightly; run 100 fixed seeds for larger synthetic layers | Any journal/status/gate/replay-digest difference is Critical: quarantine the candidate, retain the first counterexample/seed, and block merge/release. |
| Hook-enabled/disabled authority parity | Run every hook corpus case across trusted, disabled, untrusted, timeout, and failed modes whenever hook/runtime/MCP/gate code or Codex version changes | Any difference outside the allowlisted hook-observation fields, or any continuation count >1, blocks the hook/host tuple and release. |

| Panel independence | Machine-check identity/conflict/assignment/path/capability metadata for 100% of formal panels; human-inspect all signaled panels and the baseline sample in Section 7 | Any identity reuse, peer leak, early synthesis, degraded independence, or missing evidence invalidates the panel and blocks formal-review completion. |
| Stale-result rejection | Exercise every stale/cancelled/superseded/late corpus case per lifecycle change; reconcile 100% of such production receipts against accepted artifact/event hashes | Accepted stale count must remain exactly 0. Deterministic/human gates remain authoritative. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Local canonical journal + immutable assignment/proposal/artifact/decision manifests + pure status projection + content-addressed `arw.evidence` bundles, with redacted `CodexNativeExecutionAdapter` observations. This is an explicit Arize Phoenix override; no remote telemetry, tracing account, prompt export, or model-content logging is required.

The canonical trace records exact IDs, event sequence/hash chain, revisions, role/policy/schema/protocol and input/output digests, legal transition, gate state, and immutable outcome. Adapter observations are non-authoritative and record only the exact Codex/adapter/plugin/model/profile/permission identity available from the host, parent-created worker/attempt binding, start/stop/exit class, durations, token/cost fields when available, hook status/continuation count, and redacted error taxonomy. Central status/metrics must not contain source/manuscript/report text, prompts, quotations, credentials, absolute private paths, or transcripts; sensitive raw outputs stay in the per-attempt restricted evidence area and are referenced by digest.

### Key Metrics and Exact Alert Thresholds

| Signal | Threshold | Local response |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Unauthorized canonical write/attempt | `accepted_unauthorized_writes >= 1` **or** any production mutation bypass attempt detected | Critical immediately; stop dispatch, make run read-only, preserve hashes, inspect sole-writer boundary. Accepted count has a permanent target of 0. |
| Invalid contract/path/hash accepted | `accepted_invalid_proposals >= 1` | Critical immediately and quarantine run. For correctly rejected invalid proposals, warn at `>=3` in 1 hour or `>5%` of the last 20 attempts, whichever occurs first. |
| Frozen-order or replay drift | Any `authoritative_order_digest_equal == false` or `cold_replay_digest_equal == false` | Critical immediately; block transition/release and retain minimal counterexample plus both digests. |
| Stale/cancelled/superseded result | `accepted_stale_results >= 1` | Critical immediately. Correctly rejected stale results warn at `>=3` in 1 hour so the adapter/cancellation path is investigated. |
| Formal-panel independence | Fewer than 4 distinct eligible first-round identities/host IDs, unequal frozen subject/rubric digests, peer-access evidence `>=1`, synthesizer not a distinct fifth identity, or synthesis dispatched before 4 accepted reports | Critical immediately; invalidate panel and keep review gate `BLOCKED`. |
| Dissent/report preservation | Synthesis source-report coverage `<100%`, seeded/known critical-dissent recall `<100%`, or `complete` while any required/critical/`DA-critical` item is unresolved | Critical immediately; block synthesis/finalization and require scoped human resolution. |
| Retry/cancel/continuation/call loop | Any assignment `attempt_count >2`, stop-event `continuation_count >1`, formal round `host_call_count >10`, or active attempt older than `15 minutes + 10 seconds` without a terminal interruption/cancellation event | Critical immediately; deny more calls, terminate as qualified, and open retry-exhaustion/orphan decision. Warn when retry-exhaustion rate is `>5%` of the last 20 assignments. |
| Human-gate scope | Any accepted decision missing exact actor/role/rationale/scope/current hashes, releasing more than one blocker/transition, or changing prior verdict bytes | Critical immediately; reject/invalidate dependent transition while preserving original history. Every consequential decision is sampled. |
| Experiment/safety boundary | Experimental/benchmark route without distinct `experiment_designer`, experiment execution attempt `>=1`, restricted-content canary exposure `>=1`, or unapproved root/capability/network/upload use `>=1` | Critical immediately; deny action, block workflow, and route to the named domain/access authority. |
| Host qualification/provenance | Dispatch under an exact host tuple without current PASS, or missing exact stage/adapter/Codex/protocol identity in an observation | Critical before dispatch; host tuple remains unqualified. `unknown` token/cost is allowed only when explicitly recorded as unavailable, never fabricated. |
| Latency and task completion | Worker p95 `>300s` or panel-plus-synthesis p95 `>600s` over the last 20 eligible runs; expected-complete success `<95%` over the last 20 (excluding correctly expected `BLOCKED`) | Warning and capacity/adapter review. The 900-second hard attempt deadline, 10-second grace, integrity gates, and call ceilings are never relaxed to improve the metric. |

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

- Run content-free deterministic metadata checks on **100%** of assignments, proposals, canonical events, decisions, formal panels, adapter observations, and cold replays. Hashes/enums/counts/durations are sufficient for routine monitoring; no central content lake is created.
- Human-review **100%** of waivers, corrections, access/root/capability escalations, retry exhaustion/replacements, unresolved or resolved critical dissent, identity/conflict/blinding signals, stale-result anomalies, replay/order mismatch, restricted-access attempts, experiment-route exceptions, and final approvals that follow a `FAIL` or `BLOCKED` state.
- Review **100% of the first three formal panels** after any Codex, adapter, plugin-stage, model/profile, permission, role-catalog, policy, schema, rubric, or hook-digest change. Qualification does not transfer across tuples.
- From otherwise clean formal panels, select a deterministic hash-based **10% stratified sample per calendar month**, with at least 5 reviews when volume is at least 5 and all runs when volume is below 5. Stratify by workflow family, experimental/non-experimental route, reviewer role, language, evidence-access state, execution mode, and terminal status.
- Route every advisory-LLM/expert disagreement and every user/operator challenge into the human sample. Add a production-derived case only after local redaction, expert labeling, contamination review, a new immutable case ID, and assignment to a future corpus version; never feed production content to a remote judge by default.
